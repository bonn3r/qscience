About me

Updated: Agust 17, 2026

As a child, I was the kind who took apart toys to understand how they worked. My parents, noticing this curiosity, always encouraged me to read: my father gave me comic books, and my mother provided schoolbooks from various grades. My uncles, Fernando and Eliana, also played a fundamental role, gifting me books that sparked my interest in science – especially Physics. Later on, the game Half-Life, with its narrative of science fiction and speculative physics, consolidated my interest in theoretical physics. This path culminated in 2019, when I entered the Universidade Federal de Sergipe to take my first steps toward a career as a physicist.

During my undergraduate studies, I actively sought to engage in scientific initiation projects, dividing my time between the Mathematics and Physics departments. At the Department of Mathematics (DMA), under the supervision of Professor Ricardo Pinheiro, I delved into real and functional analysis across three scientific initiation projects. In the first, the professor already had a well-defined project: I studied topics in functional analysis to understand variational methods and apply them to nonlinear Partial Differential Equations (PDEs) – a tool that would later prove essential in my work with complex systems. In the following year, we designed a project tailored to my academic development. I studied an introduction to analysis in \(\mathbb{R}^n\) alongside the Real Analysis course, which I took as an elective. This experience deepened my mathematical training beyond the usual scope of a physics curriculum: I witnessed proofs, logical structures, the construction of the real numbers, and their extension to \(\mathbb{R}^n\) – a rigor I carry with me to this day. In a third project, we dedicated ourselves to an introduction to differential geometry applied to relativistic mechanics, which definitively sparked my interest in how Mathematics interacts with Physics. At the end of this study, I realized that my path would be in physics, not pure mathematics. It was at that moment that I sought out Professor Andrei Smirnov to begin my journey in mathematical physics.

At the Department of Physics (DFI), under the supervision of Professor Andrei Smirnov, I directed my research toward mathematical physics, focusing on manifolds of negative curvature and the construction of the Laplace-Beltrami operator. In this project, we deepened our study of hyperbolic manifolds and pseudo-spheres, developing a computational code in the MAXIMA platform to calculate, from any given parametrization, the metric tensor, the Christoffel symbols, the Riemann and Ricci curvature tensors, the scalar curvature, and the Laplace-Beltrami operator. This experience taught me the value of symbolic computation in theoretical physics and consolidated my interest in combining rigorous mathematical formalism with computational tools to explore fundamental problems in physics. Although the project was not completed, the journey was extremely important in preparing me for the challenges ahead.

My Undergraduate Thesis, supervised by Professor Gerson Cortês Duarte-filho, was an opportunity to apply my mathematical and computational knowledge to a concrete physics problem: the dynamics of laser systems. We studied two modeling regimes: the classical model, based on rate equations for three-level lasers, which, due to the fast transition from the third excited state to the second, can be reduced to a two-level system; and the Maxwell-Bloch equations, which combine a classical electromagnetic field with a quantum approach for the atoms. To explore these models, I developed codes in Python, validating the results with experimental data from the literature. Although nonlinear dynamics and lasers are not my primary area of focus, this experience consolidated my skills in computational modeling and complex systems analysis – competencies I intend to bring to my studies in mathematical physics and general relativity.

Outside of academic life, I am a music enthusiast. I dedicate a few hours each week to listening exclusively to full albums by my favorite artists, paying attention to the nuances, the instruments, the lyrics, and the message the artist intended to convey. I am also a brazilian jiu-jitsu (BJJ) practitioner and an avid cyclist: in BJJ, I earned my blue belt within one year and train whenever possible; in cycling, I ride about 90 km per week, trying to maintain an average of 20 km/h on a simple bike from the 2000s... as soon as I settle down in Recife, I will build myself a new bike!
